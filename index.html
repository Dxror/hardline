The Compression

A year of your life went into a manuscript. Before anyone touches it with a red pen, you need to hear that.

Thirteen chapters went in. Five came out. Not because eight were bad, but because most readers never made it far enough to find out.

  • Where's the best story sitting? Almost every manuscript has its strongest proof buried in the middle, right where it does the least good.
  • Is there a duplicate hiding in plain sight? That line you love, you've already said it, almost word for word, two chapters back.
  • Does the ending close the loop or leave it open? A tidy, resolved ending feels satisfying to write. It works against a book whose whole job is making the reader want more of you.

Get those three right and most of the rest follows on its own.

Content-to-Book

Ten hours of talking is not a manuscript. It's ten hours of you thinking out loud, saying the same idea four different ways because you didn't know which version would land. All of that is gold. None of it is finished, and none of it is doing the one job that actually matters.

Most podcast-to-book services just clean up your transcript. Chapter one is episode one, chapter two is episode two, tidied up, some paragraph breaks added, done. Technically a book. Functionally a very expensive PDF nobody's going to finish.

We don't start with your episodes in order. We start by finding the one idea you keep circling back to across all ten hours, the belief you'd defend even if every guest disagreed with you, and build the book's spine around that first. Because that spine is what eventually gets quoted in an introduction before you walk on stage.

Questions worth answering.

What if the writing was never the problem?+

Most authors assume a book that isn't converting readers into clients needs better sentences. Almost every time I open a manuscript, the sentences are fine. The sequence is what's working against the author.

Do I need a finished manuscript?+

No. An outline, rough draft, 50 hours of podcast content, or 300 episodes works. We assess what's there and build from it. The diagnostic tells us if you have enough raw material, or if you need to create more before we start.

Do you take royalties?+

No. The book is 100% yours. Hardline Editions appears on the copyright page as the imprint, but you keep all rights and all revenue. I charge a flat fee for the work. What the book earns for your business, speaking fees, coaching clients, consulting engagements, is yours alone.

How long does it take?+

Diagnostic: 15 minutes. Assessment: 48 hours. The Finish Line: 10 days from kickoff to print-ready files. Authority Engine: 4 weeks including the full platform build. Timelines assume you can turn around feedback within 24 hours.

Is this self-publishing?+

Technically yes, we use KDP and IngramSpark for printing and distribution. Strategically no. Most self-published books are built as products for sale. Yours is built as a business asset. The printing method is the same. The strategy, structure, and outcome are completely different.

What's the difference between The Finish Line and Authority Engine?+

The Finish Line gets you a professional book. Authority Engine turns that book into a client acquisition system. If you already have a funnel and just need the asset, The Finish Line is enough. If you need the book to actually generate leads and appointments, you need the Engine.

What if I'm not ready?+

I'll tell you. The diagnostic is honest. If a book isn't the right move, maybe you need to refine your offer first, or build more case studies, I'll say so and point you toward what is. No charge, no hard sell. I'd rather you come back ready than start prematurely.
